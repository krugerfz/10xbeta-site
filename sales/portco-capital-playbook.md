# 10XBeta Portfolio Company Capital Playbook

> **Purpose:** Stage-by-stage operational guide for capitalizing 10XBeta portfolio companies. Use to identify the right channels, sources, and approach for each portco based on where it sits in the lifecycle.
>
> **Companion to:** `sales/capital-strategy.html` (strategic presentation), `sales/SESSION_LOG.md` (session work record), `sales/portco-plans/` (per-portco capital plans).

## Short-term operational focus (clarified Session 008)

Multiple stages exist in this playbook because portcos span the full lifecycle. But **operationally right now, the active work is seed-stage capital** for currently-active portcos (RedZone and Validose as of Q2 2026; more will follow). The Series A+ channels, placement-agent relationships, Track 2 studio raise, and Track 3 fund are medium-to-long-term work and shouldn't compete for execution attention against the active seed closes.

Day-to-day priority order:

1. **Active seed closes** — RedZone, Validose, any other portco currently raising.
2. **Strategics-at-seed relationships** — strategic CVCs (J&J Innovation, BD Strategic Investments, pharma CVCs, defense prime CVCs) as seed investors AND future-acquirer relationship anchors. This collapses Track 1c into Track 1a.
3. **Series A+ relationship-building** — placement agents, larger CVCs. Build for when portcos hit that scale; not active execution today.
4. **Track 2 prep** — background work for the 10XBeta studio priced round.
5. **Track 3** — fund prep gated on signal.

## The Lifecycle: Six Stages

### Stage 0 — Pre-Formation (in-studio)

- **Duration:** 0 to 6 months
- **Capital:** studio internal funding only
- **What gets done:** Opportunity Engine identifies the gap. Lab tools build POC. IP module files provisionals. Market analysis and regulatory pathway scoping.
- **Output to next stage:** validated concept, initial IP filed, market analysis, ready to spin out
- **Best fit for grants.** Long grant cycles match the long incubation cycle. Apply for SBIR Phase I and indication-specific grants here, where time horizons align.

### Stage 1 — Pre-Seed ($250K to $1.5M)

- **Duration:** 6 to 18 months post-spinout
- **What gets done:** full POC, file utility patents, hire first team member outside studio (CEO or technical lead), initial regulatory strategy, early user / clinical interviews
- **Output to next stage:** POC working end-to-end, IP portfolio established, regulatory pathway identified, founding team in place

**Primary sources (equity-first if portco is in market-push mode):**
- **Strategic CVCs that write seed checks** — J&J Innovation (sub-$1M), Olympus Innovation Fund, BD Strategic Investments (sometimes), pharma CVCs for drug-delivery plays, defense prime CVCs for dual-use plays
- **Studio equity** (10XBeta as anchor)
- **Sector-specific angel investors** (10XBeta's network)
- **SPV-based syndicate** (AngelList or law-firm structured)
- **Accelerators** — MedTech Innovator, IndieBio, MATTER

**Grants (background, not primary):**
- SBIR Phase I ($150K-$300K) if filed early; will land 6-12 months later as runway extension
- Foundation grants for specific indications if applicable

**NOT placement agents.** Ticket size doesn't justify their fees.

**Channel:** direct outreach, studio network warm intros, SPV close

**Approach:** SAFEs or convertible notes typically. Priced rounds rare at this stage.

### Stage 2 — Seed ($1.5M to $5M)

- **Duration:** 12 to 24 months
- **What gets done:** V1 prototype that works in real-world conditions, bench data, possibly animal data, regulatory strategy formalized, engineering team built out, manufacturing strategy
- **Output to next stage:** clinical-grade prototype, regulatory submission readiness, clear FDA pathway, validated commercial hypothesis

**Primary sources (equity-first for market-push portcos):**
- **Strategic CVCs as seed investors (priority for medtech)** — pharma CVCs (Pfizer Ventures, Eli Lilly Ventures, GSK, Novartis VF, Sanofi, AbbVie, Bayer Leaps, AstraZeneca), defense prime CVCs (Lockheed Martin Ventures, RTX Ventures), medtech CVCs (J&J Innovation, BD Strategic Investments, Olympus Innovation Fund)
- **Pre-seed / seed medtech VCs** — BioInnovation Capital, LifeForce Capital, Medical Innovation Partners, KCK Group, Genoa Ventures, DCVC Bio
- **Hardware-friendly seed funds** — Bolt VC, Hardware Club, Lux Capital, 8VC, Day One Ventures
- **Defense-tech VCs** for dual-use medtech — Founders Fund, Marlinspike, Razor's Edge, Shield Capital
- **SPV-based syndicate close** with angels filling syndicate

**Grants (background, runway extension only for market-push portcos):**
- SBIR Phase II ($1M-$2M) if Phase I is in hand and timing fits
- Existing federal relationships continue producing value (e.g., RedZone's ARPA-H, SOCOM)
- ARPA-H, BARDA program awards if engagement already exists
- Do NOT time the round to grants for active portcos. 6-12 month cycles don't match market push.

**Sometimes placement agents** start to be relevant at the upper end ($4M-$5M raises). Still rare. Default: no placement agents at seed.

**Channel:** mix of warm intros and direct outreach, SPV close, strategic CVC direct relationships

**Approach:** priced seed round typical. Lead investor plus syndicate of 3-8 follow-on. SPV mechanism for known-LP syndication.

### Stage 3 — Series A ($5M to $20M)

- **Duration:** 12 to 24 months
- **What gets done:** clinical trials (if applicable), FDA submission and clearance pursuit, team scaling, early commercial preparation, manufacturing partnership or in-house build
- **Output to next stage:** regulatory clearance or pathway near completion, clinical data, early commercial signal, scaled team

**Primary sources:**
- **Medtech-focused VCs** — HealthQuest Capital, Polaris Partners, F-Prime, Versant Ventures, Sofinnova, Catalyst Health, ATV, USVP healthcare practice
- **Larger strategic CVCs** — Medtronic Ventures (typical entry), Boston Scientific Strategic Investments, J&J Innovation at Series A, Stryker Corp Dev
- **Generalist VCs with medtech books** — Lux Capital, Bessemer, Khosla Ventures, NEA, a16z Bio
- **ARPA-H Phase II** ($5M+ if milestone-based engagement exists)

**Placement agents become relevant** — Outcome Capital, Leerink Partners, HC Wainwright start being useful for round formation, especially $10M+ raises.

**Approach:** institutional Series A. Lead investor + 2-5 follow-on. Term sheet quality matters more than headline valuation.

### Stage 4 — Series B ($15M to $50M)

- **Duration:** 12 to 18 months
- **What gets done:** commercial launch post-FDA clearance, broader clinical validation, sales force build, geographic expansion
- **Output to next stage:** commercial revenue, market validation, expansion runway

**Primary sources:**
- Growth-stage medtech VCs (NEA, a16z Bio, General Catalyst Health, Frist Cressey, Foresite, Cormorant, Casdin)
- All big-eight strategic CVCs viable at this stage
- Late-stage healthcare focused (Cormorant Asset Management, Casdin Capital, Foresite Capital)

**Placement agents are central** — Outcome Capital, Leerink, HC Wainwright, plus larger banks (Piper Sandler, Jefferies, William Blair) for $25M+.

**Secondaries become possible** — integrated tender offers (Lane 2 in Track 1b).

### Stage 5 — Series C and Growth ($30M to $100M+)

- **Duration:** 12 to 24 months
- **Primary sources:** late-stage / growth equity (TPG Growth, KKR Health Care, Bain Capital Life Sciences, Carlyle, General Atlantic), crossover investors (Fidelity, T. Rowe Price, BlackRock, Wellington), strategic acquirers as investors
- **Placement agents and full-service IBs** — Leerink, Piper Sandler, Jefferies, William Blair, plus bulge-bracket Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan
- **Organized tender offers** at this stage for studio liquidity

### Stage 6 — Exit (M&A or IPO)

- **M&A advisors / bankers:** Edgemont Capital Partners (10XBeta has live relationship — they're 80% M&A), TM Capital, Solomon Partners, Cain Brothers / KeyBanc, plus full-service IBs
- **Strategic acquirers:** big-eight medtech (Track 1c relationships that started at seed pay off here)
- **IPO underwriters** (less common for medtech hardware): Leerink, Morgan Stanley, Goldman, J.P. Morgan, Piper Sandler

## Cross-Cutting Strategies

### Non-Dilutive Capital (Grants) — right tool for the right stage

**Where grants fit well:**
- **Stage 0 incubation** (in-studio R&D) — long cycles match long discovery cycles
- **Stage 1 pre-seed** if portco can afford to be patient — apply early in parallel with founding work
- **Existing in-flight grant relationships** at any stage — they continue producing runway value once landed
- **Specific milestone funding** at Series A (e.g., ARPA-H Phase II for clinical trials, BARDA milestone payments)

**Where grants don't fit:**
- **Active seed-stage portcos in market-push mode.** 6-12 month application cycles don't match a portco trying to close a round and get to market in 3-4 months.
- **Series B+ as primary capital.** Equity is the right mechanism at scale.
- **Any portco where grant timing would gate market entry.** Grants are good when they're runway extension, not when they're load-bearing.

**Programs to know (for incubation work and Stage 1 pre-seed when patient):**

Federal: SBIR / STTR (NIH multiple ICs, DoD CDMRP, SOCOM Tactical Medicine, AFWERX, NavalX, NSF), ARPA-H ($5M-$50M per program), BARDA, NIH program awards, DoD BAA / OTA.

Foundations: Bill & Melinda Gates (global health), Wellcome Trust, Robert Wood Johnson, Helmsley Charitable Trust (T1D, IBD), Doris Duke Charitable, Patrick J. McGovern (AI-health).

State / regional: NYSTAR, MassChallenge HealthTech, CALSEED.

**Recommended studio capability:** a fractional grants writer for the studio's incubation pipeline and selectively for existing-relationship continuation. Not a tool to staff for active seed-close work.

### The Studio's Angel Network

For 10XBeta specifically, the studio's angel network is a strategic asset that should be built deliberately. Target: 15-25 named angels who:
- Have prior medtech / hardware / regulated environment experience
- Write $25K-$100K checks
- Move fast (days, not weeks)
- Provide ongoing referrals
- Are kept warm with quarterly updates

**Who fits:**
- Founders of prior medtech exits in 10XBeta's network
- Ex-strategic execs (Medtronic, J&J, Boston Scientific, Stryker, Abbott, BD)
- Ex-FDA officials transitioning to industry
- Healthcare-focused operators (HCA, Kaiser, Mayo, Cleveland Clinic alumni)
- Hardware-experienced operators (Tesla, Apple, Anduril, SpaceX)
- Defense / federal-aligned operators (RedZone-relevant)

**Operating mechanism:**
- Maintain a tracking list (CRM or markdown file)
- Quarterly newsletter or update letter
- Annual in-person event (lab visit, demo day, JPM dinner)
- "First look" privilege on new portcos before broader fundraising

### SPV-Based Close Mechanism

**For pre-seed and seed rounds**, an SPV (special purpose vehicle) under a 506(b) private configuration is the right close mechanism for known-LP syndication. 10XBeta has the AngelList syndicate (slug: 10xbeta) already configured with RedZone seed as an SPV underneath.

**Mechanics (per Session 003 notes):**
- 506(b) self-certification (not third-party verification) appropriate for known-LP networks
- SPV can close below target but above minimum floor
- Single close then wire (not rolling access)
- Multiple-close availability to be confirmed directly with AngelList

**Apply this pattern across pre-seed and seed portcos.** Set the floor and target per portco; close once the lead VC commits + strategic CVCs sign + angels syndicate to floor.

### Platform OS as Capital Lever

10XBeta's Platform OS workflow (Opportunity Engine → Lab → IP) is itself a capital strategy lever:

1. **Reduces pre-seed capital need.** Studio-funded pre-formation work produces POC + IP before spinout, so external pre-seed capital starts further along.
2. **De-risks seed and Series A.** Stronger IP portfolio, better technical de-risking, faster path to data.
3. **Differentiates the studio's pitch** to strategics (Track 1c) and to studio investors (Track 2).
4. **Enables strategic-funded de-risking deals** where a CVC pays 10XBeta to build POC #X with option-to-acquire terms.

Pitch Platform OS as the studio's productized R&D capability in every external conversation. It's the answer to "why does a studio model work."

## Decision Frameworks

### When to apply for grants vs raise equity

- **Stage 0 incubation:** always apply for grants. Time horizons match.
- **Stage 1 pre-seed for not-rushed portcos:** apply for grants in parallel.
- **Active seed-stage portcos in market push:** equity first, grants as background only.
- **For defense / biodefense / public-health indications with active federal traction:** grants continue contributing but don't time the round to them.
- **For commercial-only indications:** grants are runway extension, not foundation.

### When to bring in placement agents

- **Not before Series A.** Their economics don't work below ~$10M.
- **Sweet spot:** $10M+ raises where their fee is justified.
- **Build relationships now** (Session 001 outreach), bring them into the process when a portco hits that scale.

### When to engage strategics

- **Strategic CVCs that write seed checks (J&J Innovation, BD, pharma CVCs, defense prime CVCs):** at seed, as anchor investors. Collapses Track 1c into Track 1a for these portcos.
- **Innovation arms (JLABS, Stryker Connected, etc.):** any stage. Lower bar, no "are you selling" signal.
- **Corporate dev / strategic ventures (later-stage):** Series A or later.
- **Acquisition conversations:** Series B or later, with proven traction.

### When to do secondaries

- **Lane 1 (incubated, majority equity):** 3+ years post-formation with clear company traction. Accept 10-20% discount.
- **Lane 2 (minority positions):** Series B+ when integrated tenders are normalized. At-round pricing.
