# 10XBeta Portfolio Company Capital Playbook

> **Purpose:** Stage-by-stage operational guide for capitalizing 10XBeta portfolio companies. Use to identify the right channels, sources, and approach for each portco based on where it sits in the lifecycle.
>
> **Companion to:** `sales/capital-strategy.html` (strategic presentation) and `sales/SESSION_LOG.md` (session work record).
>
> **Why this exists:** Capital sources change dramatically by stage. Pre-seed and seed portcos are funded by grants, angels, and early-stage VCs. Series A+ portcos are funded through different channels including placement agents. Treating them the same is the most common mistake studios make, and it wastes relationship capital on firms that can't help. This playbook is the corrective.

## The Lifecycle: Six Stages

### Stage 0 — Pre-Formation (in-studio)

- **Duration:** 0 to 6 months
- **Capital:** studio internal funding only
- **What gets done:** Opportunity Engine identifies the gap. Lab tools build POC. IP module files provisionals. Market analysis and regulatory pathway scoping.
- **Output to next stage:** validated concept, initial IP filed, market analysis, ready to spin out
- **Why this matters:** external investors at pre-seed want to see something already built. The studio's Platform OS produces that for free, before any external pitch. This is a 10XBeta differentiator that should be priced into the studio's value proposition.

### Stage 1 — Pre-Seed ($250K to $1.5M)

- **Duration:** 6 to 18 months post-spinout
- **What gets done:** full POC, file utility patents, hire first team member outside studio (CEO or technical lead), initial regulatory strategy, early user / clinical interviews
- **Output to next stage:** POC working end-to-end, IP portfolio established, regulatory pathway identified, founding team in place

**Primary sources:**
- Studio equity (10XBeta as anchor)
- Sector-specific angel investors (10XBeta's network)
- Government grants — SBIR Phase I ($150K-$300K), some early Phase II concept awards
- Accelerators — MedTech Innovator, IndieBio, MATTER
- Strategic CVCs that go early — rare but possible (J&J Innovation has done sub-$1M checks)

**NOT placement agents.** Ticket size doesn't justify their fees (5-7% on $500K = $30K, won't get attention).

**Channel:** direct outreach, studio network warm intros, grant applications, accelerator applications

**Approach:** SAFEs or convertible notes typically. Priced rounds rare at this stage. Studio takes founder shares; external capital takes preferred stock or note positions.

### Stage 2 — Seed ($1.5M to $5M)

- **Duration:** 12 to 24 months
- **What gets done:** V1 prototype that works in real-world conditions, bench data, possibly animal data, regulatory strategy formalized (510(k) vs De Novo vs PMA), engineering team built out, manufacturing strategy
- **Output to next stage:** clinical-grade prototype, regulatory submission readiness, clear FDA pathway, validated commercial hypothesis

**Primary sources:**
- Pre-seed / seed medtech VCs — BioInnovation Capital, LifeForce Capital, Medical Innovation Partners, KCK Group, Genoa Ventures, DCVC Bio
- Hardware-friendly seed funds — Bolt VC, Hardware Club, Lux Capital (sometimes early), 8VC, Day One Ventures
- Strategic CVCs at seed — J&J Innovation, Medtronic Ventures (sometimes), BD Strategic Investments, Olympus Innovation Fund
- Government grants — SBIR Phase II ($1M-$2M), ARPA-H program awards, BARDA (biodefense indications)
- Foundation grants for specific indications — Gates Foundation (global health), Wellcome Trust, RWJF, Helmsley
- Angel syndicates — Astia Angels, sector-specific angel networks

**Sometimes placement agents** start to be relevant at the upper end ($4M-$5M raises). Still rare.

**Channel:** mix of warm intros and direct outreach, grant writing, syndicate building

**Approach:** priced seed round typical. Lead investor plus syndicate of 3-8 follow-on.

### Stage 3 — Series A ($5M to $20M)

- **Duration:** 12 to 24 months
- **What gets done:** clinical trials (if applicable), FDA submission and clearance pursuit, team scaling, early commercial preparation, manufacturing partnership or in-house build
- **Output to next stage:** regulatory clearance or pathway near completion, clinical data, early commercial signal, scaled team

**Primary sources:**
- Medtech-focused VCs — HealthQuest Capital, Polaris Healthcare, F-Prime, Versant Ventures, Sofinnova Partners, Catalyst Health Ventures, ATV (Advanced Technology Ventures)
- Larger strategic CVCs — Medtronic Ventures (typical entry), Boston Scientific Strategic Investments, J&J Innovation, Stryker Corp Dev
- Generalist VCs with medtech books — Lux Capital, Bessemer Venture Partners, Khosla Ventures, NEA, a16z Bio
- Government grants for substantial programs — ARPA-H Phase II ($5M+), BARDA milestones, DoD medical technology programs

**Placement agents become relevant** — Outcome Capital, Leerink Partners, HC Wainwright start being useful for round formation, especially $10M+ raises.

**Channel:** placement agent process + direct outreach + mutual investor intros

**Approach:** institutional Series A. Lead investor + 2-5 follow-on. Term sheet quality matters more than headline valuation.

### Stage 4 — Series B ($15M to $50M)

- **Duration:** 12 to 18 months
- **What gets done:** commercial launch post-FDA clearance, broader clinical validation, sales force build, geographic expansion, possible adjacent indication work
- **Output to next stage:** commercial revenue, market validation, expansion runway

**Primary sources:**
- Growth-stage medtech VCs — NEA, a16z Bio, General Catalyst Health, Frist Cressey Ventures, T. Rowe Price (crossover), Janus Henderson
- Strategic investors at scale — all big-eight CVCs become viable, plus regional strategic partners
- Late-stage healthcare focused — Cormorant Asset Management, Casdin Capital, Foresite Capital

**Placement agents are central** — Outcome Capital, Leerink, HC Wainwright, plus larger banks (Piper Sandler, Jefferies, William Blair) for $25M+.

**Secondaries become possible** — integrated tender offers (Lane 2 in Track 1b) at this stage.

**Channel:** banker-led process is standard

**Approach:** institutional Series B. Often a "growth round."

### Stage 5 — Series C and Growth ($30M to $100M+)

- **Duration:** 12 to 24 months
- **What gets done:** commercial scale, broader indication expansion, international expansion, manufacturing scale, potential IPO preparation
- **Output to next stage:** exit-ready scale, broader market position

**Primary sources:**
- Late-stage / growth equity — TPG Growth, KKR Health Care, Bain Capital Life Sciences, Carlyle, General Atlantic
- Public market crossover investors — Fidelity, T. Rowe Price, BlackRock, Wellington Management
- Strategic acquirers showing interest as investors (not full M&A yet)

**Placement agents and full-service IBs** — Leerink, Piper Sandler, Jefferies, William Blair, plus bulge-bracket Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan for $50M+.

**Organized tender offers** at this stage for studio liquidity.

**Channel:** full-service IB led process

### Stage 6 — Exit (M&A or IPO)

- **M&A advisors / bankers:** Edgemont Capital Partners (10XBeta has live relationship — they're an exit specialist, 80% M&A), TM Capital, Solomon Partners, Cain Brothers / KeyBanc, plus full-service IBs
- **Strategic acquirers:** from big-eight medtech (Track 1c relationships pay off here)
- **IPO underwriters** (less common for medtech hardware): Leerink, Morgan Stanley, Goldman, J.P. Morgan, Piper Sandler

## Cross-Cutting Strategies

### Non-Dilutive Capital (Grants)

Critical for medtech because regulated environments are capital-intensive and grants are large. Programs to know:

**Federal:**
- **SBIR / STTR** — NIH (multiple ICs), DoD (CDMRP, SOCOM Tactical Medicine, AFWERX, NavalX), NSF
  - Phase I: $150K-$300K (proof of concept)
  - Phase II: $1M-$2M (prototype development)
  - Phase III: commercialization (not SBIR per se, but commercial follow-on awards)
- **ARPA-H** — $5M-$50M per program, breakthrough medtech
- **BARDA** — biodefense, pandemic preparedness, public health
- **NIH program awards** — R01, R44, U01, etc. for academic-aligned work
- **DoD** — BAA (Broad Agency Announcements), OTA (Other Transaction Authority), very large checks possible

**Foundations:**
- Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation (global health)
- Wellcome Trust (UK, global)
- Robert Wood Johnson Foundation (US health systems)
- Helmsley Charitable Trust (type 1 diabetes, IBD)
- Doris Duke Charitable Foundation
- Patrick J. McGovern Foundation (AI-health intersection)

**State / regional:**
- NYSTAR (New York), MassChallenge HealthTech, CALSEED (California), various other state innovation programs

**Why this matters operationally:**
Grants extend equity capital. A portco that lands $1M SBIR Phase I + $500K ARPA-H exploratory + $500K foundation grant has $2M of non-dilutive capital that effectively extends its seed runway by 12-18 months without dilution. This is the most leveraged capital available.

**Recommended studio capability:**
A dedicated grants writer or grants strategy advisor. Can be fractional. Worth the cost — even one major SBIR award ($1M+) pays for years of this role. RedZone proves 10XBeta can land federal money; that capability should be a repeatable studio function across the portfolio.

### Strategic CVCs Across Stages

Strategic CVCs invest at multiple stages, often with different investment teams and check sizes per stage. Knowing which CVC plays where:

| CVC | Typical stage | Check range |
|---|---|---|
| J&J Innovation / JJDC | Seed through Series B+ | $500K-$10M |
| JLABS (J&J) | Pre-seed / seed residency | No equity, free workspace |
| Medtronic Ventures | Series A+ | $3M-$15M |
| Medtronic Hugo Innovation | Early-stage scouting | Variable, partnership focus |
| BD Strategic Investments | Series A+ (sometimes seed) | $3M-$10M |
| Boston Scientific Strategic Investments | Series A+ | $5M+ |
| Boston Scientific Connected | Early-stage scouting | Partnership focus |
| Stryker Strategic Investments | Series A+ | $5M+ |
| Stryker Connected | Early-stage scouting | Partnership focus |
| Abbott Strategic Investments | Series A+ | $5M+ |
| Edwards Lifesciences | Series A+ (narrow focus) | $3M-$10M |
| Baxter Ventures | Series A+ | $3M-$10M |
| Olympus Innovation Fund | Seed and Series A | $1M-$5M |

**Operational implication:** different CVCs are appropriate for different stages. J&J Innovation may be the only big-eight strategic that writes a seed check. Don't pitch Medtronic Ventures at pre-seed.

### The Studio's Angel Network

For 10XBeta specifically, the studio's angel network is a strategic asset that should be built deliberately. Target: 15 to 25 named angels who:
- Have prior medtech / hardware / regulated environment experience
- Write $25K-$100K checks
- Move fast (days, not weeks)
- Provide ongoing referrals
- Are kept warm with quarterly updates

**Who fits:**
- Founders of prior medtech exits in 10XBeta's network
- Ex-strategic execs (Medtronic, J&J, Boston Scientific, Stryker, Abbott, BD)
- Ex-FDA officials transitioning to industry
- Healthcare-focused operators (HCA, Kaiser, Mayo, Cleveland Clinic alumni)
- Hardware-experienced operators (Tesla, Apple, Anduril, SpaceX)
- Defense / federal-aligned operators (RedZone-relevant)

**Operating mechanism:**
- Maintain a tracking list (CRM or markdown file)
- Quarterly newsletter or update letter
- Annual in-person event (lab visit, demo day, JPM dinner)
- "First look" privilege on new portcos before broader fundraising

### Platform OS as Capital Lever

10XBeta's Platform OS workflow (Opportunity Engine → Lab → IP) is itself a capital strategy lever:

1. **Reduces pre-seed capital need.** Studio-funded pre-formation work produces POC + IP before spinout, so external pre-seed capital starts further along.
2. **De-risks seed and Series A.** Stronger IP portfolio, better technical de-risking, faster path to data.
3. **Differentiates the studio's pitch** to strategics (Track 1c) and to studio investors (Track 2).
4. **Enables strategic-funded de-risking deals** where a CVC pays 10XBeta to build POC #X with option-to-acquire terms.

Pitch Platform OS as the studio's productized R&D capability in every external conversation. It's the answer to "why does a studio model work."

## Decision Frameworks

### When to apply for grants vs raise equity

- **Always apply for grants in parallel** with equity raising at pre-seed and seed. Grants take 6-12 months; can't be primary capital path but extends runway materially.
- **For defense / biodefense / public-health indications:** grants can be primary (RedZone, PARADIGM model). Equity raised later, on better terms, against grant-funded milestones.
- **For commercial-only indications:** grants are extension, not foundation.

### When to bring in placement agents

- **Not before Series A.** Their economics don't work below $10M.
- **Sweet spot:** $10M+ raises where their fee is justified.
- **Build the relationships now** (Session 001 outreach), bring them into the process when a portco hits that scale.

### When to engage strategics (Track 1c)

- **Innovation arms (JLABS, Stryker Connected, etc.):** any stage. Lower bar, no "are you selling" signal.
- **Corporate dev / strategic ventures:** Series A or later.
- **Acquisition conversations:** Series B or later, with proven traction.
- Strategics often invest first, acquire later. Investment is a relationship-builder.

### When to do secondaries (Track 1b)

- **Lane 1 (incubated, majority equity):** 3+ years post-formation with clear company traction. Accept 10-20% discount.
- **Lane 2 (minority positions):** Series B+ when integrated tenders are normalized. At-round pricing.

## Operating Cadence

- **Monthly:** portco capital review at studio level. Where is each portco in stage? What's next? What's needed?
- **Quarterly:** portfolio update to angel network and strategic CVC contacts. One page.
- **Quarterly:** grant pipeline review. What's applied for, what's pending, what's due.
- **Annual:** playbook refresh. Capital sources change. New funds emerge. Stale entries get removed.
