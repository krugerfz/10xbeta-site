# 10XBeta Lab Revenue Strategy

> **Purpose:** Generate paying lab work from non-portco clients to de-risk studio revenue. The studio needs $500K/month in external client revenue to break even. Currently zero. This workstream is the studio's revenue path.
>
> **Companion to:** `sales/portco-capital-playbook.md` (portco capital strategy), `sales/SESSION_LOG.md` (session record), `sales/lab-revenue-pipeline.md` (to be created when first conversations land).
>
> **Distinct from capital strategy:** The capital strategy raises money for portcos (equity in, dilution accepted). This strategy sells services (revenue in, no dilution). Same studio capabilities, different commercial mechanism.
>
> **Verification status:** University programs, VC funds, corporate innovation arms, and competitor rates named below are from training-data knowledge. Verify each is still active and current before locking outreach plans.

## Why this workstream exists

10XBeta's lab is a fixed-cost asset. Engineers (Brooklyn + Poland + South Africa), equipment, lab tools, IP-filing capability, regulatory thinking, manufacturing relationships. That capability exists whether or not it's being used.

Three ways the studio fills it:

1. **Studio incubation** (in-house portcos) - core studio activity. Equity capture mechanism.
2. **Portco follow-on work** - existing portcos buying lab time. Mixed mechanism.
3. **External client work** - paying customers who are *not* portcos. Pure revenue mechanism, no dilution.

This doc covers the third bucket. It's the one that de-risks the studio commercially.

## The break-even reality

**The studio needs $500K/month ($6M/year) of recurring external client revenue to break even. Currently zero.**

This is the operational clock the workstream runs against.

### Revenue volume math

Hitting $500K/month recurring requires sustained pipeline of:

- ~6 active T&M engagements at ~$83K/month each ($1M/year each), or
- ~12 active fixed-scope projects at ~$50K/month recognized each ($600K each spread over 12 months), or
- A mix landing the same monthly total

Single large contracts shift the math significantly. One $2M annual retainer covers 40% of break-even alone. Anchor clients matter disproportionately.

### Realistic build curve from zero

| Month | State | Monthly revenue |
|---|---|---|
| 1-4 | Outreach, scoping, proposals out | $0 |
| 5-6 | First engagement closes | $50K-$150K |
| 7-9 | 2-3 active engagements | $150K-$300K |
| 10-12 | 4-5 active engagements | $300K-$450K |
| 13-15 | 6+ active engagements | $500K break-even |

Critical assumptions:
- First-close timing: 4-6 months from kickoff. University channel fastest, large medtech slowest.
- Engagement ramp: 1 new close every 1-2 months sustained.
- Engagement duration: 6-12 months average, recognized monthly.
- Close rate on qualified pipeline: ~25-30%.

If first-close timing slips to 8 months, break-even pushes to 18+ months. The first close is the gate. Closing fast on a smaller contract beats closing slow on a larger one.

### Cash runway implication

The 12-15 month build to break-even means the studio burns cash against the lab cost base during ramp. This workstream essentially races the studio's runway. Track 2 (studio priced round) is the parallel capital path. **Running both in parallel is the right move.** Whichever lands first wins the race; ideally both contribute.

## Competitive position

### The cost advantage: SA + Poland engineering

10XBeta has engineering teams in South Africa and Poland in addition to the Brooklyn lab. This is a material cost structure advantage versus US-only competitors (Veranex, Sagentia, Cambridge Consultants, TTP, Velentium, Battelle).

**Approximate fully-loaded engineering cost by region:**

| Talent base | Annual loaded cost | Effective hourly cost |
|---|---|---|
| US senior engineer | $200K-$300K | ~$120-$180 |
| US junior engineer | $130K-$180K | ~$80-$110 |
| Poland senior engineer | $80K-$120K | ~$50-$75 |
| Poland junior engineer | $50K-$80K | ~$30-$50 |
| South Africa senior engineer | $50K-$80K | ~$30-$50 |
| South Africa junior engineer | $30K-$50K | ~$18-$30 |

A blended team mixing US (project lead, IP, regulatory) with Poland (engineering core) and South Africa (production engineering, testing) delivers outcomes at meaningfully lower cost than US-only competitors.

### Competitor rate intel (verify before locking)

Approximate US-market rates for medtech contract development:

| Firm | Blended hourly | Senior strategy / IP |
|---|---|---|
| Cambridge Consultants | $400-$600 | $600-$800 |
| Sagentia (Science Group) | $300-$500 | $500-$700 |
| TTP (The Technology Partnership) | $350-$500 | $500-$700 |
| Veranex (KKR) | $250-$400 | $400-$600 |
| Battelle | $250-$400 | $400-$600 |
| Smithwise / Recom | $250-$400 | $400-$550 |
| Velentium | $200-$300 | $300-$450 |
| Smaller boutiques | $200-$350 | $300-$500 |

These ranges are training-data approximate. Verify with industry contacts before locking pricing. Recent RFP responses from these firms (if accessible) give exact current numbers.

### Three pricing approaches

**Approach A: Undercut to win.** Bill $180-$250/hr blended. Significantly below US competitors. Wins price-sensitive deals and grows pipeline volume fast. Margin still healthy on offshore-heavy team mix.

**Approach B: Match and capture margin.** Bill $300-$400/hr blended, slightly below US competitors. Same effective price to client, much higher gross margin. Premium positioning. Better when relationship and quality matter more than price.

**Approach C (recommended): Hybrid.** Lead with value pricing at $250-$300/hr blended that's below US competitors but enough margin to invest in growth. Reserve undercut pricing ($200-$250) for strategic-anchor clients who unlock channel access (e.g., a flagship university partnership or first big VC anchor relationship). Reserve premium pricing ($350-$400) for clients where price isn't the gate.

### ITAR / clearance constraint on offshore teams

Offshore teams (Poland, South Africa) generally cannot work on ITAR-controlled or classified defense work. This narrows the defense-tech channel:

| Defense work type | Offshore allowed? |
|---|---|
| Defense-tech operators (Anduril, Shield AI, etc.) commercial dev | Usually yes at early stages |
| Defense primes (Lockheed, RTX, Boeing, Northrop) | Usually no - ITAR controlled |
| DoD-direct contracts (SBIR Phase III, OTAs) | No - US-only |
| RedZone-style federal work | No - US-only |

For defense channel work that requires US-only teams, 10XBeta competes without the offshore cost advantage. Pricing matches US competitor range. Volume in this channel is constrained by US-only team capacity. Margin pressure is lower because all competitors face the same US-only constraint.

### Capability differentiators (beyond cost)

Competing on price alone with established dev firms is risky. The cost advantage matters most when paired with capability differentiation:

1. **Platform OS productized R&D.** Opportunity Engine validates the problem. Lab tools build the POC. IP module files claims. This is a *system*, not just a team for hire.
2. **Studio velocity.** We ship in weeks because we built the studio to ship in weeks.
3. **Operator mentality.** Team has spun out portcos. We think like founders, not consultants.
4. **IP fluency.** We file, we read claims, we design around. Many dev firms outsource IP and lose the engineering-IP feedback loop.
5. **Brooklyn Navy Yard location.** Lab tours land for NYC tech, finance, academic ecosystems.
6. **Portfolio IP leverage.** We bring proprietary capabilities (e.g., from Spiro Wave, RedZone, Validose work) to client engagements that pure-services firms don't have access to.

Lead with these in every pitch, alongside competitive pricing. Don't sell on price alone, but don't ignore that we can credibly undercut on price when needed.

## Channel sequencing

Given the $500K/month break-even clock, channels are prioritized by speed to first revenue:

| Channel | Accessibility | Scale | Repeatability | Sales cycle | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Universities with VC funds | HIGH | LOW-MED | HIGH | 3-6 mo | **First wave (immediate)** |
| VCs investing in early-stage medtech | MED | MED-HIGH | HIGH | 6-12 mo | **First wave (immediate)** |
| Defense tech (operators, non-ITAR) | MED | MED | MED | 4-9 mo | **Second wave (within 60 days)** |
| Large medtech outsourced development | LOW | HIGH | MED | 6-18 mo | **Long-cycle (plant seeds now, harvest later)** |
| Defense primes (ITAR) | LOW | MED | MED | 6-18 mo | **Long-cycle / opportunistic** |

**Why this sequencing given the break-even clock:**

- Universities = fastest path to first revenue. Brooklyn Navy Yard proximity to NYC universities is real geographic edge. Start here for first-close timing.
- Early-stage medtech VCs = highest-repeatability path. Relationship-building runs ahead of first engagement (6-12 months) but compounds because each VC firm represents multiple portcos. Build in parallel from day one.
- Defense-tech operators (non-ITAR) = decent cycle speed, manageable scale, RedZone signal supports the story.
- Large medtech and defense primes have the largest contracts but cycles too slow to drive break-even by month 15. Worth starting now to land revenue in months 18+.

## Engagement models across channels

**Fixed-scope project (most common, ~70%):** SOW defines deliverables, milestones, payment schedule. Best for clients who know what they want.

**Time and materials (~20%):** day rate or hourly. Best for exploratory work or ongoing engagement with shifting scope.

**Retainer arrangements (target: ~5-10% but high leverage):** monthly fee for ongoing access to lab capacity. One $1M-$2M annual retainer with an anchor client moves break-even math significantly.

**Equity-for-services (rare, ~5%):** 10XBeta takes equity in client company in lieu of full payment. Only when 10XBeta would invest as angel anyway.

**Option-to-license (~5%, large medtech specialty):** 10XBeta develops on contract; client gets option to acquire resulting IP or company.
