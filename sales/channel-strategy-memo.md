## 1. What we are selling (capability baseline)

Two distinct product lines come out of the lab. Every offering recommendation below points to one or both.

**Line A — Productized medtech development.** One vertically integrated team, ISO 13485, four capability layers (Hardware, App, Platform, AI) plus Manufacturing. Sold as four phases, each shaped backward from the client's exit:

- **Phase 01 Pre-Seed Readiness** — $60-140K, 6-8 wks, TRL 1→3. IP/FTO, business + financial model, regulatory pathway memo, reimbursement strategy, investor deck.
- **Phase 02 Seed Readiness** — $280-560K, 12-16 wks, TRL 3→5. Functional POC device, 510(k) predicate analysis, KOL validation, provisional patent, updated investor materials.
- **Phase 03 Series A Readiness** — $840K-1.75M, 24-32 wks, TRL 5→7. Beta hardware, 510(k) submission package, 3 buyer LOIs, manufacturing partner, Series A materials.
- **Phase 04 Exit Readiness** — $280-560K, 12-16 wks, TRL 7→9. Clearance, manufacturing transfer + first run, first pilot, acquirer/licensee mapping, diligence-ready data room.

The differentiator: five vendors (CDMO + consultancy + design + regulatory + accelerator) replaced by one team, ~30% lower capital to clearance, ~2x faster (≈14 mo vs 36), client keeps 100% equity and all IP.

**Line B — Custom AI tools / bespoke software (10XLAB AI).** Workflow→UI translation + embedded AI agents + hardware-grade software discipline, built for any business's daily operations (sales, ops, finance, vendor, knowledge work). Client owns it when we leave. This is the Platform-OS capability sold outward. Relevant for non-medtech corporates and services businesses that will never buy a device program.

**Sprint-sized entry points** (for fast, single-signoff first revenue): Phase 01, or a scoped 5-day paid scoping engagement off the "pick one concept" offer. Line B can also start with a single workflow tool.

## 2. The channels — definition and why each works

We do not sell to "medtech" as one undifferentiated market. We sell through eight channels, each with a different buyer, budget, decision speed, and reason to say yes. The deck already carries tailored variants for two of them (VC/incubator, university fund); the rest extend the same logic.

### Channel 1 — Venture Capital funds (portfolio acceleration)
**What it is:** Device/health/hardware VCs whose portfolio companies need build execution. We do not ask the fund to buy; we become the vetted dev partner they route portcos to.
**Why it works:** Their medtech returns come from acquisitions, and we build for that exit shape — no equity conflict, an explicit fence around their equity. Milestones hit ~2x faster at ~30% lower full-stack cost makes their capital go further, which is the fund's own interest. Engage via a portfolio-wide MSA, pilot on one portco, expand. *(Deck slide 17 is the ready variant.)*
**Decision speed:** Partner intro is fast (days–2 wks warm). The revenue event is the portco's signature, not the fund's — faster than it looks.

### Channel 2 — University funds / Tech Transfer Offices (TTOs)
**What it is:** University venture funds and TTOs sitting on disclosures that are bolt-on acquisition candidates but not yet productized.
**Why it works:** We turn a disclosure into an acquisition-ready asset. Phase 1 deliverables their investment committee can actually underwrite, at ~30% less than the consultant/firm/lawyer stack. Engage via a TTO-level MSA with pre-negotiated terms, diligence partnership for fund underwriting. *(Deck slide 18 is the ready variant.)*
**Decision speed:** Slower (academic + IC cycles), but high volume of disclosures per relationship. A patient channel that compounds.

### Channel 3 — Corporate Venture Capital (CVC)
**What it is:** The venture arms of the big strategics (Medtronic Ventures, JJDC, BD Strategic Investments, Lockheed Martin Ventures, etc.).
**Why it works:** Two doors. (a) Same portfolio-referral logic as Channel 1 — which portco needs execution. (b) A relationship-warming path to the parent's business units. CVCs increased early-stage allocations through 2025-26; they want their bets to hit milestones, which is exactly what we sell.
**Decision speed:** Referral conversations fast; parent-BU spend slow (committee).

### Channel 4 — Strategics / corporate business-unit R&D
**What it is:** The operating-unit advanced-development teams inside the big-eight medtech (and defense primes) — the people with real project budgets.
**Why it works:** We are productized R&D studio capacity they can rent without adding headcount, matched to a franchise hook (drug delivery→BD, respiratory/critical care→Medtronic/Edwards, connected care→Abbott/Baxter). This is where direct lab spend actually lands.
**Decision speed:** Slowest cold (procurement committees) — *not* a sprint channel. Fast once warm at OU level. Persistent, high-ticket, long-cycle.

### Channel 5 — Funded medtech startups with outsourced/thin R&D
**What it is:** Seed-to-Series-B device companies — often software/clinical founders — with no full hardware bench, or a capacity/specialty gap.
**Why it works:** This is the bullseye. They have a fundable inflection ahead and cannot justify full-time hardware headcount pre-scale. We get them to the next round faster than hiring would. Founder has signature authority. Highest sprint conversion.
**Decision speed:** Fastest — founder signoff, no committee.

### Channel 6 — Defense / dual-use hardware (RedZone-adjacent)
**What it is:** Mid-size and earlier defense-tech companies, plus government dev-funded programs (ARPA-H, DHA SBIR/STTR, DIU, SOCOM).
**Why it works:** Manufacturing-at-scale is the stated 2026 pain point across defense tech — squarely our Manufacturing layer. Our SOCOM/ARPA-H/Spiro lineage is direct proof. Government programs are dev dollars (grant paperwork, not commercial SOW) with a credibility halo. ITAR work routes to the US team only.
**Decision speed:** Commercial mid-size fast; government programs slow but fundable. Leverage existing RedZone contacts.

### Channel 7 — Pharma / drug-delivery / combination products
**What it is:** Pharma and biotech needing device or delivery engineering outside their core competency.
**Why it works:** Drug delivery is a proven competency (Validose). Combination products, autoinjectors, wearable injectors, decentralized-trial hardware, companion-dx readers — all full-stack fits. Lead with the delivery wedge.
**Decision speed:** Medium; specialty-pharma faster than big pharma.

### Channel 8 — Corporate innovation / services businesses (Line B door)
**What it is:** Non-medtech corporates probing a health/device adjacency, and services businesses that need custom AI/software, not a device.
**Why it works:** Matches the two engagements already in flight (consumer-products CEO + services-business founder). Line A for the adjacency-testers (Phase 01 feasibility as low-commitment entry); Line B (custom AI tools) for the services businesses that will never build a device but will buy bespoke software with an agent layer.
**Decision speed:** Fast — single corporate sponsor, discretionary innovation budget.
