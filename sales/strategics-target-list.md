## How a strategic actually engages a studio like 10XBeta

Three distinct doors at every large medtech/defense company. They are run by different people with different budgets:

1. **Corporate venture (CVC)** — invests equity in startups. Door to portfolio referrals ("which of your portcos needs hardware execution?"), not direct lab spend.
2. **External innovation / incubator** — scouts and partners with early-stage tech. Door to collaboration mandates and sometimes direct-funded dev.
3. **Business-unit R&D / advanced development** — the operators with real project budgets. This is where productized-R&D-studio spend actually lands. Slowest to reach cold, fastest to convert once warm.

The pitch (per Session 004): **productized R&D studio capability** — OE → Lab → IP as a repeatable service, not individual POCs.

## TIER 3 — Companies that outsource device/hardware development

The broader "who outsources" question. These are buyers of exactly what the lab sells. Segment by why they outsource:

### A. Funded medtech startups without in-house hardware teams
- **Profile:** Seed-to-Series-B device companies, software/clinical founders, no mechanical/EE/firmware bench.
- **Why they outsource:** Can't justify full-time hardware headcount pre-scale; need a POC or DfM before next round.
- **Where to find:** VC portfolios (F-Prime, Lux, 8VC, Founders Fund, Bolt — already named in capital conversations). MedTech Innovator cohort companies. JLABS residents.
- **Fit:** Bullseye for S1/S4 sprints. **[FAST — founder signoff]**
- **This is the warm-pool category #2 lever — highest sprint conversion.**

### B. Digital-health / SaaS companies adding a hardware or device component
- **Profile:** Software-native companies that suddenly need a sensor, wearable, or regulated device.
- **Why they outsource:** Zero hardware DNA; regulatory pathway is alien to them.
- **Fit:** S1 feasibility + S2 regulatory pathway. The "we don't know what we don't know" buyer.

### C. Pharma / biotech needing device or delivery components
- **Profile:** Drug-delivery, combination products, companion diagnostics, decentralized-trial hardware.
- **Why they outsource:** Device engineering is outside core competency.
- **Fit:** Drug-delivery (Validose lineage) is the wedge. S1/S2/S4.
- **Where to find:** Pharma CVCs (named in Track 1c), combination-product teams.

### D. Corporate innovation arms testing adjacencies
- **Profile:** Non-medtech corporates (consumer, industrial) probing a health/device adjacency.
- **Why they outsource:** Want to test before building internal capability.
- **Fit:** S1 feasibility as the low-commitment entry. Maps to the two engagements already in flight (consumer-products + services-business clients in the WIP deck).

### E. Established device companies with overflow / specialty gaps
- **Profile:** Mid-size device makers whose internal teams are at capacity or lack a specific skill (optics, embedded, AI).
- **Why they outsource:** Capacity overflow or a one-off specialty need.
- **Fit:** S4/S5. Offshore cost advantage (SA + Poland) is the differentiator here.
