## Why no placement agents or investment banks

Same stage logic as RedZone. Seed-stage raises sit below placement-agent economic threshold; their fees can't be earned at $2M-$4M. Correct exclusion, not a gap. Use the five-tier channel approach below.

## Capital strategy: five-tier approach

### Tier 1: Pharma corporate VCs (highest fit channel)

Drug delivery sits directly adjacent to pharma's core business. Pharma CVCs have explicit thesis fit, write seed checks, and have strategic motivation to invest (drug-delivery innovation extends their molecule franchises).

- **Pfizer Ventures** — early-stage drug delivery
- **Eli Lilly Ventures** — does seed for delivery platforms
- **GSK Ventures**
- **Novartis Venture Fund**
- **Sanofi Ventures**
- **AbbVie Ventures**
- **Merck Global Health Innovation Fund**
- **Bayer Leaps** — often early, can write larger checks
- **AstraZeneca Innovation**
- **Boehringer Ingelheim Venture Fund**

This is likely Validose's single highest-fit investor category. Direct outreach + warm intros via pharma operator network.

### Tier 2: Drug delivery / medtech device seed VCs

- **BioInnovation Capital**
- **F-Prime Capital**
- **DCVC Bio**
- **Sofinnova Partners** (early-stage practice)
- **Sands Capital** (life sciences ventures)
- **Lux Capital**
- **Andera Partners** — drug delivery focus
- **Polaris Partners** (sometimes seed)

### Tier 3: Strategic medtech CVCs with drug-delivery interest

- **BD Strategic Investments** — highest fit; BD is a leading drug-delivery device company. Direct outreach worth prioritizing.
- **J&J Innovation** — broad fit, writes seed checks
- **Olympus Innovation Fund**
- **Insulet** — corporate development, less of a venture arm but watches the space
- **Tandem Diabetes Care** — corporate development

### Tier 4: Non-dilutive (significant for adherence indications)

Depending on Validose's specific clinical indication, multiple non-dilutive sources fit:

- **NIH SBIR Phase I and II** — adherence research is well-supported by NIH
  - NIDA (substance use adherence)
  - NHLBI (cardiovascular adherence)
  - NIDDK (diabetes adherence)
  - NIAID (HIV / infectious disease adherence)
  - NCI (oncology adherence)
- **ARPA-H** — medication adherence is a stated agency priority area
- **Foundation grants depending on indication:**
  - JDRF (type 1 diabetes adherence)
  - Helmsley Charitable Trust (T1D, IBD)
  - American Diabetes Association
  - American Heart Association
  - Patient-advocacy foundations specific to the lead indication

### Tier 5: Angels

- Pharma executives (commercial + device-side operators)
- Drug-delivery industry operators (ex-Insulet, ex-Tandem, ex-DexCom, ex-Medtronic Diabetes)
- Indication-specific angels in the lead clinical area

## Connectors who can open these doors

- Pharma corporate VC partners — surprisingly accessible directly on LinkedIn
- Drug-delivery accelerators / programs (MedTech Innovator has had drug delivery cos in cohorts)
- BD Strategic Investments direct relationship — highest strategic fit, worth a dedicated path
- Indication-specific operator networks (e.g., diabetes operator community if T1D is the lead)
- The fractional Head of Capital Strategy when hired (Session 006 priority)

## 90-day sequence

**Days 1-30:**
- Pharma CVC outreach (the priority channel). 8-10 firms. LinkedIn + warm intros where available.
- BD Strategic Investments direct outreach (highest strategic fit).
- NIH SBIR Phase I application if not already filed.

**Days 31-60:**
- Drug-delivery / medtech device VC outreach (Tier 2). 5-7 firms.
- Foundation grant application for the lead indication.
- Angel outreach to pharma operator network.

**Days 61-90:**
- Tier 3 strategic CVCs that didn't respond in days 1-30 (J&J Innovation, Olympus).
- Round close with committed capital from CVCs + grants extending runway + angels filling syndicate.
